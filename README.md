# FedRAMP Rev5 to FR20X High-Level Overview

For CSPs with legacy FedRAMP Rev 5 authorizations that are currently strategizing a move to FR20X, the path forward can feel overwhelming.

Especially when it comes to KSIs versus traditional controls. What do these KSIs truly mean? How do they map to your current control implementations?

Before you deep-dive into the technical details, I'd encourage you to take a step back and understand what the current FedRAMP Program team is actually trying to accomplish.

FedRAMP has always been a path for the U.S. Government to use Commercial Cloud Offerings. The key word in that sentence is "Commercial." What happened in practice was CSPs building a separate, FedRAMP-specific offering. For many, that offering didn't get the same attention as their commercial product, and it didn't maintain feature parity. Many factors contributed to this, but the primary one was too many hoops to jump through for approval of new cutting-edge features.

FR20X is bringing the focus back to what the program was always meant to be: the U.S. Government using commercial clouds. The Authorization Data Sharing process makes this clear, framing security and compliance information as the intellectual property of the CSP and emphasizing that the federal government benefits when that same security information is shared broadly for maximum transparency and accountability.

They've also updated the definition of Federal Customer Data to reinforce this shift. It now explicitly excludes account information, service metadata, analytics, telemetry, and similar data generated by the CSP itself.

For Rev 5 CSPs moving to FR20X, the core mindset shift is this: your CSO is not a government system. It is your commercial system. Build it with the security lens of all customers in mind. The KSIs are simply your proof of your security and privacy architecture.
